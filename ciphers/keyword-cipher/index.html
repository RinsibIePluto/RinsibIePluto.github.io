Keyword cipher

A keyword cipher is a substitution cipher with an easy-to-remember key. Instead of memorising a whole scrambled alphabet, you remember one word, and the alphabet is built from it.

Difficulty in Cyrune: ★★★★☆

A little history

Keyed alphabets were popular for centuries because they solve a real problem: a fully random substitution alphabet is hard to remember and dangerous to write down. A keyword lets two people rebuild the same alphabet from memory. The trade-off is that the alphabet is less random, which helps codebreakers.

How it works

  1. Write the keyword's letters, dropping any repeats.
  2. Continue with the rest of the alphabet in order, skipping letters you've already used.
  3. Line this alphabet up under the normal one and swap each message letter for the letter beneath it.

How to crack it

A keyword cipher can be attacked like any substitution cipher, by counting letters and spotting common words. But it has an extra weakness: after the keyword, the alphabet runs in order. Letters near the end of the alphabet often encrypt to themselves or to a neighbour, and once you've found a few letters you can often guess the keyword and fill in everything else.
