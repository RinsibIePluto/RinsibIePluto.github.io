Polybius square

The Polybius square writes the alphabet into a 5 × 5 grid and turns each letter into two numbers: its row and its column. It's one of the oldest ways to turn letters into something that can be signalled.

Difficulty in Cyrune: ★★★★☆

A little history

The Greek historian Polybius described the square around 2,200 years ago as a way to signal over distance with torches: a number of torches on the left for the row, and on the right for the column. The same idea came back as the tap code, which prisoners have used to talk through cell walls, most famously American prisoners of war in Vietnam, tapping the row and then the column.

How it works

Write the alphabet into a 5 × 5 square. There are 26 letters and only 25 cells, so I and J share one. Each letter becomes its row number followed by its column number.

12345
1ABCDE
2FGHI/JK
3LMNOP
4QRSTU
5VWXYZ

To add secrecy, the square can start with a keyword. With the keyword CRANE, the square begins C R A N E, and the same message becomes 43 41 31 15 43.

How to crack it

Each pair of digits always stands for the same letter, so a Polybius cipher is really a substitution cipher in disguise, and it falls to letter counting. The numbers give extra clues: letters that share a first digit sit in the same row of the square, and with a keyword square, the later rows still run in alphabetical order.
